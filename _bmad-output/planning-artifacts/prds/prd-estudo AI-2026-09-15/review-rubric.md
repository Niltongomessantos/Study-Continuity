# PRD Quality Review - Study Continuity Local-First Web App

## Overall verdict

Ready for downstream UX, architecture, and story work for the declared hobby/personal Fast-path scope. The PRD has a coherent local-first thesis, explicit MVP boundaries, testable functional requirements, and usable cross-references. The session-validity rule is now consistent: every session uses 50% of planned duration, regardless of duration.

## Decision-readiness - strong

The Local Domain authority, local agenda, no-notification rule, and post-MVP Google Calendar boundary are stated as decisions. The rule conflict is surfaced rather than hidden.

### Findings
- **resolved** Session validity (§4.3, FR-8) - the user confirmed that 50% of planned duration is the only completion threshold; the 30-minute value was illustrative only.

## Substance over theater - strong

The vision, journeys, glossary, and requirements are specific to study continuity. The document avoids generic productivity and boilerplate NFR claims.

## Strategic coherence - strong

Features support the thesis of making the next study step, timing, material, and outcome visible while keeping external platforms outside the domain.

## Done-ness clarity - strong

FR-1 through FR-15 include observable consequences, and the high-level acceptance criteria cover the primary local lifecycle and agenda boundaries.

## Scope honesty - adequate

Non-goals, deferred work, assumptions, risks, and open questions are explicit. Added assumption tags now surface provisional title and local recurrence ownership.

## Downstream usability - strong

UJ-1 through UJ-4, FR-1 through FR-15, and SM identifiers are contiguous and cross-referenced. Glossary terms are used consistently and journeys have a named protagonist.

## Shape fit - strong

The depth is appropriate for a personal MVP while still supporting UX, architecture, and story planning.

## Mechanical notes

No broken IDs or unresolved cross-references found. The PRD contains the required Essential Spine plus relevant cross-cutting constraints, security, persistence, integration, and risk sections.
